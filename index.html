Research boundary

Publication standard

A result is not yet a record.

Publication waits until the result can be read beside the conditions that produced it. A second run in another environment gets its own provenance instead of inheriting the first result.

  1. DateWhen this exact version entered the public ledger.
  2. Environment and versionsThe hardware, runtime, model, libraries, and services that shaped the run.
  3. HypothesisA bounded claim written so that the evidence can disprove it.
  4. MethodThe sequence, inputs, comparison points, controls, and stop rules used.
  5. Observed resultThe output of the run, reported without extending beyond what was directly seen.
  6. Evidence statusWhether the entry is observed, reproduced, inferred, inconclusive, or still unverified.
  7. LimitationsThe gaps, conditions, failure modes, and unknowns that narrow the result.
  8. ArtifactsSafe logs, code, fixtures, captures, or sources that let a reviewer inspect the claim.
  9. Next testThe single follow-up most likely to strengthen, narrow, or overturn the record.

Publication boundary

Useful evidence, without unsafe exposure.

Publishable

  • Public-safe methods, dummy data, sandbox fixtures, and reproducible test conditions.
  • Observed failures, inconclusive results, stated uncertainty, and sanitized artifacts.

Excluded

  • Client, school, private, production, credential, or provider-sensitive data.
  • Commercial intake, beginner curriculum, unsupported impact claims, and unverified event schedules.
